AI Is Not Neutral

How AI and technology reproduce inequality while appearing neutral.

Explore the Issue

The New Jim Code

In Race After Technology, Ruha Benjamin uses the term “New Jim Code” to explain how modern technology can reproduce discrimination while appearing neutral or progressive. The phrase plays off the history of Jim Crow laws, showing that inequality can continue through newer systems like algorithms, data collection, artificial intelligence, and automated decision-making.

Why Algorithms Aren’t Neutral

Biased Data

AI systems are trained on data from the real world, which means they can reflect existing racial, gender, economic, and social inequalities.

Hidden Decisions

Many automated systems make decisions in ways that are difficult for everyday users to see, understand, or challenge.

False Neutrality

Technology is often trusted because it seems scientific or objective, but human choices still shape how these systems are built and used.

Recent Examples

These recent examples show how Benjamin’s argument still applies to today’s technologies.

Example 1: AI Hiring Tools

Recent article:UW research finds racial and gender bias in AI tools ranking job applicants’ names

AI hiring systems are often promoted as objective ways to evaluate applicants, but recent studies found that some programs favored resumes associated with white male applicants over women and Black applicants. This shows how bias can become hidden within automated systems that appear neutral and efficient.

Connection: This connects to the New Jim Code because hiring technology can make biased decisions look objective.

Example 2: Facial Recognition

Recent article:More than a Dozen Wrongful Arrests Due to Police Reliance on Facial Recognition Technology

Facial recognition technology has led to multiple wrongful arrests after people were incorrectly identified by AI systems. Critics argue that these technologies disproportionately affect marginalized communities and increase surveillance in already over-policed areas.

Connection: This shows how technology can increase surveillance and harm people unevenly.

Example 3: Healthcare Algorithms

Recent article:How Health Care Algorithms and AI Can Help and Harm

AI systems are increasingly used in healthcare to make recommendations and organize patient care. However, researchers warn that these systems can reflect existing healthcare inequalities and may provide unequal treatment or access to care for certain groups.

Connection: This matters because systems that appear scientific can still reproduce inequality.

Example 4: Generative AI Bias

Recent article:Generative AI: UNESCO study reveals alarming evidence of regressive gender stereotypes

Recent studies found that generative AI systems often reproduce harmful racial and gender stereotypes through text and image generation. Instead of removing social bias, these systems can reinforce existing assumptions found in the data they were trained on.

Connection: This connects to the unit because AI reflects the data and social values behind it.

Why This Matters

The main issue is not just that technology sometimes makes mistakes. The deeper concern is that these mistakes often follow social patterns that already exist. When technology is treated as neutral, it becomes harder to question who benefits, who is harmed, and whose experiences were left out of the design process.

“Big Data processes codify the past. They do not invent the future.” Cathy O’Neil, quoted in Race After Technology by Ruha Benjamin
